feat: the /loopsec work list has a soft ceiling

A list of 200 items is 200 rounds, and EVERY ONE of them is "productive" because it examines
something — so the plateau never fires and the run goes quiet for hours with no warning.
Measured earlier: the share of rounds spent enumerating is 1/(k+1) for a sweep of k items, so
the cost is not the cap itself, it is the COMMITMENT the cap bounds.

SOFT, and that is the whole design.

Refusing the item the model just found loses information — and an item it cannot record is an
item the run will never come back to. So `recordSurface` at the ceiling:

    SURFACE AT 60 ITEMS (soft ceiling 60). It was recorded — refusing it would lose it — but
    the run is now committed to at least 41 more round(s).
    Analyse before enumerating further: `sec_surface` again only for a place that matters MORE
    than what is already waiting.

It states the COMMITMENT (more rounds), not just the count, because "60 items" is abstract and
"41 more rounds" is not.

Once the list is at the ceiling the surface brief STOPS ASKING FOR A SWEEP and says why:

    **THE LIST IS ALREADY AT 60 ITEMS** (soft ceiling 60), and every one of them costs a round.
    Do NOT sweep again for its own sake. `sec_surface` only for a place that matters more than
    what is already waiting — the analysis is what produces findings, and a list nobody finishes
    produces none.

The analyze brief carries the same fact at the point where the model would grow the list, which
is where it can act on it — the surface brief is only read during enumeration.

60 rather than 30: the brief asks for 10-30, and a ceiling at the top of the requested range
would warn on a conforming sweep. 60 is ~2x headroom, so it fires on the genuinely unbounded
case and not on the intended one.

FOUR tests, including one asserting the ceiling and the dig budget are NOT the same knob — one
bounds how much there is to do, the other how long one thing may take, and conflating them
would make the run impatient about the wrong thing.
