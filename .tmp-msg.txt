feat: a falsified probe can no longer be silently overridden

THE ONE HARD CHECK IN THE WHOLE SYSTEM, and it was empty.

A falsified probe means the round stated what the hypothesis predicts about one
mechanical fact and the prediction did NOT hold. The executor has always drawn the
right conclusion from that (`suggestedVerdict = "rejected"`), and
`hypothesis_record` always accepted `confirmed` anyway — because the outcome lived
only in the verify tool's RESPONSE TEXT and was gone by the next call.

Self-audit confirmed it: `verification_recorded` did not exist, `Evidence` has no
outcome field, and `setStatus` never looked at a probe. The information was not in
the tree, so nothing could be enforced.

- New `verification_recorded` event + `Hypothesis.lastVerification`, folded onto
  the node: suggested verdict, survived/falsified/inconclusive counts, and the
  counterexample lines themselves.
- `hypothesis_record` now REFUSES `confirmed` when the last verification run
  falsified the hypothesis, and it runs BEFORE the write — a check that leaves the
  status changed is not a refusal. It returns the counterexamples and the two ways
  out: record `rejected`, or pass `override` with the reason.
- It is NOT an absolute refusal. A probe CAN be wrong — and an existing test
  turned out to be exactly that case: its grep asked for `verify\(` with
  `expectation: "present"` on a hypothesis that claims verify() is ABSENT, so the
  counterexample was an artefact of how the probe was written. That test now
  demonstrates the override path, which is what the override is for.
- `Hypothesis.falsificationOverride` records the reason and the counterexamples it
  overrode, so an override is a CLAIM that gets printed rather than a silent
  bypass.

ALSO FIXED, found while doing (1):

`requires: []` was unreachable. I split "no gates recorded" from "gates assessed
and there are none" two commits ago, but nothing ever told the model how to say
the second one — so every self-contained finding (a hardcoded secret, an exposed
debug endpoint) would report as "利用前提未评估" forever. The output requirements
now say to write `requires: []` explicitly.
