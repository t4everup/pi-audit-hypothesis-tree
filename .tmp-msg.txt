feat: /loopsec can go DEEPER on an item, and the brief finally says what just happened

Three things the operator asked for, all against the mode's own goals.

1. DEPTH — an item may take more than one round.

Before this, an item got ONE round. A round spent genuinely tracing something that needed a
second pass recorded nothing and moved no status, so `evaluateRound` called it unproductive
and it BURNED A PLATEAU SLOT. The model paid for being thorough, and the cheap way out was to
close the item hastily — the wrong incentive, and the opposite of what an audit wants.

    sec_dig S-0003        # "I am not finished with it — give me another round"

- comes back NEXT round, ahead of the plan AND ahead of a promoted item. Finishing a trace
  you started beats starting a more promising-looking one: an abandoned trace is wasted work,
  an unstarted item is still just an item.
- a depth round counts as PROGRESS, so it does not burn the plateau.
- budgeted at 2, reusing `pursueRounds` — the same decision the hypothesis mode makes, so one
  knob and `0` disables depth in both. Past the budget `sec_dig` is refused and the refusal
  names both ways out: "a depth round that never ends is the same as never deciding."
- the budget is spent by the LOOP on prepare, like `pursueSpent`, so a crash cannot hand out
  unlimited rounds. Past it the item drops back to plan order rather than holding the queue.
- a CLEARED item cannot be dug: a clearance names a guard, and depth does not undo it.

2. THE BRIEF NOW CARRIES THE LAST ROUND AND THE STALL COUNTER.

Neither existed in ANY sec brief. The stall counter is the only self-correction signal the
mode has — without it a run ends for a reason the model never learned, and it sits at 7/8 with
no idea. And `ALREADY CLEARED` was cut at `slice(-12)`, so past twelve clearances the oldest
reasons fell out of the brief and the model could not tell why an area had been ruled out; it
is now cut at 40 and STATES the total, so what is missing is visible rather than inferred.

One subtlety worth recording: `snapshot.loop.stallRounds` is the count from BEFORE this tick's
evaluation, because tickLoop evaluates the round it was handed and only then prepares the next
one. The brief was reading the stale snapshot and printing a counter that was always one round
behind — the exact number the line exists to communicate. The live loop is passed separately,
the way the hypothesis branch already does it.

3. THE SIXTH FIELD THE STORE ALLOWLIST HAD TO LEARN, and the first that landed in the WRONG
   NORMALIZER.

`...(typeof o.surfaceId === "string" ...)` appears in BOTH `normalizeFinding` and
`normalizeRoundRecord`, and `replace(..., 1)` took the earlier one. `RoundRecord.surfaceWasDigging`
was written to the log and never read back, so a depth round still burned a plateau slot and
the feature was inert. The agent found it by MEASURING rather than reading: the debug trace
showed `digRounds` incrementing correctly while the record's flag stayed `undefined`.

Caught by a test only after the test itself was fixed: the first version left EVERY item
cleared, so the next round was a SURFACE round, which carries no ALREADY CLEARED section at
all — the assertion would have passed without testing anything.
