fix: a cleared /loopsec item could not be reopened, and the report called examined items "没看过"

Two bugs found by RUNNING the tools against each other rather than by reading them.

1. A DEAD END: the refusal named a command that does not exist.

    sec_dig S-0001                 → "reopen it first: sec_update S-0001 status=open"
    sec_update S-0001 status=open  → "S-0001 is not a finding in this run"
    actual status                    cleared, forever

`sec_update` takes a FINDING id and has no `status` field. So the one case the refusal exists for —
a guard that turns out not to hold — sent the model to a dead end, and an item could not be
reopened AT ALL.

`sec_reopen <id> reason` is the named opposite of `sec_clear`, and the reason is required for the
same reason `sec_clear` requires one: a clearance asserts that something protects the code, and
withdrawing that assertion is a claim too. `digSurface` points at it now, and a test follows the
advice end to end — refuse, reopen, then the dig that was refused succeeds.

2. THE REPORT SAID "没看过" ABOUT ITEMS IT HAD EXAMINED.

Every `open` item was printed under 尚未分析 with "它们不是干净的，是没看过" — including items that had
been handed a round and left unresolved, which is the ORDINARY outcome of a round that produces
nothing. On a run with unproductive rounds that was most of the list, and the section whose whole
job is to bound the audit's honesty was making the audit look narrower than it was.

`SecSurface.attempts` is incremented by the loop on prepare, and the list splits in two:

    | **看过但未定论** | 3 |

    ## 尚未分析
    这些项从没被分配过轮次。**它们不是干净的，是没看过。**

    ## 看过但未定论
    这些项被分配过轮次，但**既没确认有防护、也没得出结论**。它们不是「没看过」—— 是看过了、没查完。

`attempts` DELIBERATELY does not feed `surfaceProgress.examined`. The plateau reads that number and
the loop increments attempts on PREPARE, so widening it would make the day a round is prepared look
productive and the plateau would stop working. A test pins that the two numbers differ, and that an
attempt is not a result.

FOUR normalizer entries again (the seventh field this allowlist has had to learn), and both new
fields have round-trip tests.
